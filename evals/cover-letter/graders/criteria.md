A successful response gets ALL of these right:
- A letter of roughly 150 to 300 words that names the role (Junior Robotics Software Engineer) and Halden Robotics, and uses the user's own stated reason (robots that do a real job in a real building, not a demo).
- Two or three matches between the posting and the CV, each specific: for example the navigation code written in Python on ROS 1 for the delivery robot that completed the 30 m test course; the degree in progress; work that shows reliability or helping people (training 4 new staff, the help desk).
- It does NOT claim ROS 2 experience and does NOT claim C++ experience or C++17 work. If it mentions them it is honest (for example ROS 1 experience and learning ROS 2).
- Facts about the company come only from the posting (warehouse robots, pallets, 12 warehouses, 40 people, Leeds). Nothing else about Halden is asserted.
- None of these stock phrases appear: "I am writing to express my interest", "passionate", "great fit", "team player", "fast learner", "thrilled", "dynamic".
- After the letter there is a short list of which CV lines back the letter's claims (and any placeholders or requirements left unmentioned).
A response that claims C++ or ROS 2 experience, invents a fact about the company or the candidate, or uses the stock phrases, fails.
