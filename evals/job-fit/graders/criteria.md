A successful response gets ALL of these right:
- It lists the posting's requirements split into must-haves and nice-to-haves and gives a verdict per requirement in a table, with the CV line that supports each.
- C++ (must-have): NOT simply "met". It appears only in the skills list with no evidence of use, so it is marked partly / thin evidence / unknown, and the response asks Sam where C++ was used.
- ROS 2 (must-have): marked partly or not met, because the CV shows ROS 1 (Noetic) only. It is not marked met.
- Degree: noted as in progress (expected July 2026), not as completed.
- Python: met, with the project as evidence. Experience with real robots: met or partly, from the delivery robot project. 1+ years of professional experience: not met (it may note this is a nice-to-have and often soft for junior roles, without counting the cafe or the volunteer role as professional robotics experience).
- It notes the role is on site in Leeds as a practical condition to check.
- It gives a clear verdict (apply, stretch or skip) with the reason, uses a count or plain words rather than a percentage match score, and says what to do about each gap that matters.
- It asks at most three questions.
A response that marks C++ or ROS 2 as fully met, or that gives a percentage fit score, fails.
