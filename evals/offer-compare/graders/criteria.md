A successful response gets ALL of these right:
- Pay is put on the same basis with the arithmetic shown: Offer B is 2,450 x 12 = 29,400 pounds a year; Offer A is 31,000. The 1,500 signing bonus is treated as one-off money, separate from salary (a first-year total of 30,900 for B may be shown alongside).
- The share options in Offer A are given NO cash value; they are listed as an unknown with questions to ask (how many, vesting, strike price or similar).
- No tax or take-home pay is estimated.
- A side-by-side table covers pay, holiday (25 against 28 days), location (on site in Leeds against fully remote), start or probation terms, with "not stated" where an offer is silent.
- It takes the user's first priority seriously: learning from senior engineers cannot be judged from what either offer says, so the response says the comparison is open on that point and lists specific questions to ask each employer (who they would work with, mentoring, team size, how a remote junior is supported).
- It does not simply declare a winner on pay. It says what the known facts favour and what answer would tip the choice, and leaves the decision to the user.
- Nothing about either company is asserted beyond what the user gave.
A response that gets the arithmetic wrong, puts a value on the share options, estimates tax, or picks an offer on pay alone, fails.
