# CV review

Input: the user's CV, pasted or attached. If they named a target (a role, a field, a country), review against it. If not, ask once what they are applying for; if they do not say, review for an entry-level role in the field the CV points to and state that assumption.

## The rule of this desk
The CV's facts belong to the user. Never add a skill, a tool, a title, an employer, a date, a grade or a number they did not give. Where a line would be stronger with a detail that is not on the page, write a placeholder such as `[add: how many users]` and ask. A made-up number in a CV is a lie the user has to defend in an interview.

## Output, in this order
1. **The thirty-second read**: who this person is, what they can do and at what level, as a recruiter skimming would take it. If that is unclear or undersells them, say so first: it is the main finding.
2. **Line by line**: a table `line (quoted) · problem · rewrite`. Look for:
   - a duty where a result belongs ("responsible for…") and results with no size or outcome;
   - claims with nothing behind them ("team player", "hard-working", "passionate", "fast learner");
   - the strongest item buried: for an entry-level CV that is usually a project, a thesis or an internship, and it belongs near the top with detail;
   - vague or inflated wording, inconsistent dates and formats, typos;
   - length: one page is the norm at entry level unless the user's country or field expects otherwise.
   Each rewrite uses only what is on the page, with `[add: …]` where a real detail is needed.
3. **Skills with no evidence**: every skill in the skills list that appears nowhere else on the CV. For each: where could it be shown (which project or job used it)? If nowhere, it should be asked about in an interview-safe way or removed.
4. **What is missing**: for an entry-level applicant, check for projects with what the person themselves did, links to code or a portfolio, the tools actually used, relevant coursework or a thesis, and contact details. Ask about each one that is absent; do not assume it exists.
5. **Three changes that matter most**, in order.

Then offer to produce the revised CV with those changes, keeping every fact as given.

## Notes
- Paid work outside the field (retail, hospitality, care) is evidence of reliability, pace and dealing with people. Say what it shows; do not dress it up as something else.
- Conventions differ by country (photo, date of birth, length, "CV" against "resume"). Follow the user's country when known, and say which convention you assumed.
- Give the review in the reply. Write a file only when the user works in files and asks.
