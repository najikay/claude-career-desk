# Job fit

Input: one job posting and the user's CV. Ask for whichever is missing. The user pastes the posting; do not go looking for it on a job site unless they give you the page and you can open it.

## Steps
1. **Read the posting**: list its requirements in its own words, split into must-haves and nice-to-haves. Note the practical conditions too: location or remote, right to work, language, degree or licence, start date.
2. **Match**, one row per requirement: `requirement · must or nice · verdict · evidence from the CV (quoted) · note`.
   - `met` needs a CV line that shows it. A skill that only appears in a skills list, with nothing showing it in use, is `partly` at most: say that the evidence is thin.
   - `partly` is the honest word for the nearest thing: ROS 1 against a ROS 2 requirement, a course against professional experience, a degree not yet finished.
   - `not met` when nothing on the CV speaks to it.
   - `unknown` when the user may well have it and simply did not write it: ask.
3. **Read it as an entry-level application**: "years of experience" on a junior posting is often a wish, not a wall. Say how projects, internships and other work count toward it, without pretending they are the same thing. A real wall (right to work, a required licence, an on-site location the user cannot reach) is named as a wall.
4. **Verdict**: `apply`, `stretch` (worth applying if the named gaps are handled) or `skip`, with the reason in two sentences. Count rather than score: "6 of 8 must-haves met or partly met". Never give a percentage match; it would be invented precision.
5. **For each gap that matters**, one of: show existing evidence better (that is cv-tailor's job), close it with something small and real (say what, and how long it would honestly take), or prepare to address it in the letter or the interview.
6. **Questions**: at most three, about the `unknown` rows.

## Rules
- Nothing is added to the user's record. If the CV does not show it and the user has not said it, it is not `met`.
- The verdict is advice. The user decides whether to apply.
- Give the result in the reply.
