# Offer comparison

Input: the terms of each offer, as the user has them.

## Steps
1. **What matters to the user**, in their order. If they have not said, ask; suggest the things that count in a first job (who they will learn from, the work itself, pay, location, stability, whether the role is permanent) and let them rank. Their order sets the comparison, not yours.
2. **Pay on the same basis**, with the arithmetic shown:
   - one period for all offers (per year), in the currency given. Do not convert currencies unless the user gives a rate;
   - gross figures stay gross. Do not estimate tax or take-home pay;
   - one-off money (a signing bonus, relocation) is listed apart from recurring pay, and may be shown as a first-year total alongside;
   - a bonus "up to" a figure is listed as stated and marked not guaranteed;
   - share options or equity get no cash value unless the user supplies the terms. They are an unknown to ask about.
3. **The table**: one row per thing that matters, one column per offer; `not stated` where an offer says nothing.
4. **Unknowns to ask each employer before deciding**: the specific questions, per offer. For a first job these usually include who the user would work with and learn from, what the first six months look like, probation terms, and how the role can grow.
5. **What tips it**: on the user's own order of priorities, which offer leads on what is known, and which single unknown or priority would reverse that.

## Rules
- Do not decide for the user. Say where the comparison stands and what would change it.
- If the user's first priority cannot be judged from what the offers say, say that the comparison is open until it is answered. Do not let pay decide because pay is the only number available.
- Use only the terms the user gave. Nothing about an employer from memory.
- Give the comparison in the reply.
