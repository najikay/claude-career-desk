# Offer comparison

Input: the terms of each offer, as the user has them. With one offer, set it against the user's priorities and what they have told you of their alternatives; the same steps apply.

## Steps
1. **What matters to the user**, in their order. If they have not said, ask; suggest the things that count in a first job (who they will learn from, the work itself, pay, location, stability, whether the role is permanent) and let them rank. Their order sets the comparison, not yours.
2. **Pay on the same basis**, with the arithmetic shown:
   - one period for all offers (per year), in the currency given. Do not convert currencies unless the user gives a rate;
   - monthly pay is multiplied by 12 unless the offer or the country implies extra salaries (a 13th or 14th month); if unsure, ask. Hourly pay needs the weekly hours from the user;
   - gross figures stay gross. Do not estimate tax or take-home pay. If one figure is net and another gross, say they cannot be compared without the user's own take-home figures;
   - one-off money (a signing bonus, relocation) is listed apart from recurring pay, and may be shown as a first-year total alongside;
   - a bonus "up to" a figure is listed as stated and marked not guaranteed;
   - share options or equity get no cash value, even with terms: show only the stated facts (how many, the price, the vesting) and list the rest as unknowns to ask about.
3. **The table**: one row per thing that matters, one column per offer; `not stated` where an offer says nothing.
4. **Unknowns to ask each employer before deciding**: the specific questions, per offer. For a first job these usually include who the user would work with and learn from, what the first six months look like, probation terms, and how the role can grow. Contract clauses (a non-compete, notice, repayment of a signing bonus) are listed as questions, with the suggestion to have someone qualified read the contract.
5. **What tips it**: on the user's own order of priorities, which offer leads on what is known, and which single unknown or priority would reverse that.

## Rules
- Do not decide for the user. Say where the comparison stands and what would change it. Saying which offer pays more is a fact; do not speculate about future raises, or about what unexplained share options might add.
- If the user's first priority cannot be judged from what the offers say, say that the comparison is open until it is answered. Do not let pay decide because pay is the only number available.
- Use only the terms the user gave. Nothing about an employer, a country's norms or a job market from memory stated as fact: if you assume something (twelve salaries a year, for example), call it an assumption and tell the user to check it.
- This is not tax, legal or immigration advice.
- Give the comparison in the reply.
