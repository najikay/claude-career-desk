# Cover letter

Input: the posting, the user's CV, and the user's own reason for wanting this job. If they have not given a reason, ask once. If they still have not, leave `[your reason: one sentence on why this company or role]` in the letter. Do not invent enthusiasm on their behalf.

## Shape
- **Opening**: the role by name, and one concrete reason for applying.
- **Middle**: two or three matches. Each takes something the posting asks for and answers it with a specific piece of the CV: what the user did, with what, and what came of it.
- **A gap, if it matters**: one honest line on the nearest thing they have and what they are doing about it. Optional; never an apology.
- **Close**: when they can start or talk, and a plain sign-off.
- **Length**: 180 to 280 words. The language and form of address follow the posting.

## Rules
- Every claim about the user traces to a CV line or to something they said. Nothing is added: no skill, no tool, no result.
- Facts about the employer come only from the posting or from the user. Do not add what you remember or assume about the company.
- No stock phrases: not "I am writing to express my interest", "I am passionate about", "I believe I would be a great fit", "team player", "fast learner", "dynamic", "thrilled". Say the specific thing instead.
- Write the way a clear, capable person writes: short sentences, first person, no flattery.

## After the letter
List, in two or three lines each: which CV line backs each claim in the letter; anything left as a placeholder; and any requirement of the posting the letter deliberately does not mention.

Give the letter in the reply as one block the user can copy.
