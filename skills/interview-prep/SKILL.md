# Interview preparation

Input: the posting and the CV. Ask what kind of interview it is (a phone screen, a technical round, a behavioural round, a panel) if the user has not said; if they do not know, prepare for a first-round mix.

## Output
1. **Tell me about yourself**: an outline for 60 to 90 seconds: where they are now, the two pieces of evidence that matter for this role, why this job.
2. **Likely questions**, each tagged with the part of the posting or the CV that prompts it:
   - about the role and motivation (about four);
   - about the skills the posting names (about five), at the depth a junior interview goes;
   - behavioural (about four): working with others, a problem solved, a mistake, pressure;
   - **about the gaps** (one for each real gap between the CV and the posting's must-haves).
3. **Answer outlines**: bullet points, not scripts, each built from a named item on the CV.
4. **STAR stories**: three, from real items on the CV: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Use the user's own part in the work ("I wrote…"), not the team's. Where the CV gives no result, write `[add: what happened]`; never supply one. Say which behavioural questions each story answers. Work outside the field counts: a café rush or a help desk shows pressure, pace and people.
5. **Gap answers**: the honest form is the nearest thing I have done, what carries over, and what I am doing to close it. No bluffing: an interviewer will ask a follow-up.
6. **Questions to ask them**: five, specific to this posting and employer, drawn from what the posting says.
7. Offer a mock interview: one question at a time, feedback after each answer.

## Rules
- Answers use only what is on the CV or what the user has said. If a skill is listed with no evidence, ask the user where they used it before writing an answer about it.
- Do not predict the exact questions or promise an outcome. These are the likely ones.
- Facts about the employer come from the posting or the user.
- Give the preparation in the reply.
